4. (code runs 32-36, pass 5) Pass 4: replacing the SG number in the prompt by a wrong one raises the reference NLL by 34.6 +- 1.6 nats/text (339 -> 374); the increase is mostly in the cell (first digit of the lengths +5.5, low digits of lengths +7.8 (equal axes), angles +5.2) and is small in the coordinates (fresh first digit +4.0 of 39.9; derived first digit +2.8 of 16.2). I will test H1 causally with an edit that amplifies what the SG-reading heads carry: add to the adapter of v_proj (and separately k_proj / q rows) a low-rank term that scales the value vectors of the SG-number tokens only (projector onto the subspace of residual inputs at SG-token positions). For that I need the residual inputs at the SG tokens for many space groups: pass 5 teacher-forces 1024 prompt-only texts with hidden states at layers 12..20. Dose-response will then be read on the pass-4 set: a working amplification should increase the true-vs-wrong-SG NLL gap; it is useful only if it also lowers the true-prompt NLL of the leading digits, and finally if it raises the match rate in a paired generation pass.

7. (code runs 37-39, pass 6) Pass 5 gave the residual inputs at the SG-number tokens for 1024 prompts. At the inputs of model.layers 13, 14 and 19 (the layers of the SG-reading heads) the SG-token inputs, after removing the common mean direction of other tokens, lie mostly in a low-dimensional subspace: 8 directions hold 80% and 16 directions 88% of their variance, and that 16-d subspace holds 83% of the SG-token norm but only 3-5% of the norm of other prompt/answer tokens. So an edit of the form dW_v += eps * (W_v + dW_v) V^T V (V = top-8 directions) scales the value vectors of the SG tokens by about (1+eps) and leaves other tokens nearly untouched: it amplifies exactly what the SG-reading heads move. The result is re-factorised to rank 32 by SVD (loses 2.6-3.7% of the original v_proj adapter energy). Pass 6 scores the pass-4 texts with eps=1 on model.layers 13,14,19. H1 predicts a larger wrong-vs-true SG gap (the model relies more on the number); the edit is promising only if the true-prompt NLL of the leading digits also falls. If the true-prompt NLL rises, amplifying the SG pathway distorts rather than helps.

10. (code runs 44-49) Pass 7: no strong memorisation. Seen train crystals: leading-digit NLL 51 nats/text, calibrated; unseen train crystals 63 (fresh first digit NLL 1.43 vs 1.14, slightly over-confident); val 114. 15. (code runs 68-70) Pass 10: truncating all 196 adapter matrices to rank 16 costs almost nothing (val NLL +0.59 +- 0.16 of 339, i.e. +0.17%; seen train +1.25 +- 0.13; unseen train +0.50 +- 0.19). What it does give is free rank: up to ~16 directions per matrix can be rewritten at negligible cost, which makes it possible to write a small new circuit into the adapter instead of only rescaling existing parts. The only causal lead I have for the match rate is the randomisation result that non-top-1 draws at the coarsest structural decisions (first digit of a cell length, first decimal of the first-row coordinates of each element) lower the match probability (-0.10 +- 0.06 and -0.05 +- 0.03 per draw), while draws at symmetry-image rows and finer digits do not. 24. (code runs 85-86) The split of first-row draws into equivalent-image vs other digits shows no difference (-0.009 +- 0.013 vs -0.023 +- 0.014 within problems), so the non-canonical-start idea is not supported. A correction to my earlier reading of collisions: since symmetry operations preserve distances, if a complete orbit of element B collides with a complete orbit of element A, then B's generator is itself equally close to some listed A atom. So collisions are created at the generator (first-row) choice, not at image rows, which fits the luck result. The question becomes whether the model performs an occupancy check when it places a generator: at the last coordinate (z) of an element's first row, does it avoid digit bins that would put the atom on top of an earlier atom? If it does and merely leaks probability, the heads that implement the check are a repair target (strengthen the suppression of occupied sites); if it does not check at all, there is nothing to strengthen.

25. (code runs 86-88, pass 17) Two results define a concrete, in-scope target. (1) Occupancy sensitivity: on val references, at the z first-decimal of an element's first row, the model gives 0.048 probability to a digit bin that is completely occupied (every z in the bin within 1.0 A of an earlier atom), while the truth is there 0.9% of the time; free bins get 0.109 (truth 0.120). So the model avoids occupied sites by a factor ~2 where reality avoids them by ~13: steric avoidance exists but is far too weak. (2) Where collisions are created: in 1,024 answers of the original model, 446 have a contact below 1.0 A and in 80% of them the first offending row places a NEW orbit (first row of an element 132, later all-fresh row 161, partly fresh 63; derived rows 90); for contacts below 0.5 A (the invalid/unparseable answers, 220) it is 65%. So the dominant cause of invalid structures is the placement of a new orbit's generator too close to an atom that is already listed, and the model's check for that is weak. Hypothesis H6: specific heads implement the occupancy check (from the position that chooses the last coordinate of a new orbit they attend to earlier rows that are laterally close and suppress those rows' z digits); strengthening them would cut collisions. Pass 17 records attention of all layers on two low-symmetry references where nearly every row is a generator, to find such heads: support = a few heads whose attention from the z-decision goes preferentially to blocker rows; refutation = no head distinguishes blockers from other rows.
